2. What Piptock does

Piptock suggests a cooking time for boiled eggs from what you tell it: the kind of egg, its size, how cold it is when it goes in, how you cook it, how you like the yolk and where you are (altitude). It then runs a timer and sounds an alarm. You can also set your own time.

3. Times are estimates

Every suggested time is an estimate based on typical conditions. Real results depend on things the app cannot measure, including the real size and temperature of the egg, the pan, the amount of water, your hob, whether the water is at a full boil and the lid. Your eggs may come out softer or firmer than the app suggests. Check an egg and adjust the time using the buttons in the app. We do not promise that any egg will come out exactly as you wish.

4. Food safety

Piptock is a timer, not a food-safety tool. A soft, runny or jammy yolk is not fully cooked, and Piptock cannot tell whether an egg is safe to eat.

  • Pregnant women, babies and young children, older people and anyone with a weakened immune system should only eat runny or soft eggs if they carry the British Lion mark. If you are not sure of the eggs, or they were not produced under the British Lion scheme, cook them until the white and the yolk are both solid, which is the Hard setting.
  • Do not use cracked, dirty or out-of-date eggs. Keep eggs in the fridge and wash your hands after handling them.
  • Hot water and steam can burn you. Take care when lowering eggs in and lifting them out, and keep children away from the hob.

This follows the advice of the UK Food Standards Agency, which you should check for the latest guidance.

5. Do not rely on the alarm alone

The alarm and the Lock Screen timer depend on your phone: its volume, the silent switch, Focus and Do Not Disturb, battery, notification settings and iOS itself. Any of these can stop an alarm sounding on time, or at all. Stay close to the pan, and do not leave a hob on and unattended.
